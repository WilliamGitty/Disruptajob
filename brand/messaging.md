# Messaging

Copy you can lift. Everything here follows [voice.md](voice.md) — if you write something new, run
it past the test: *would you say this out loud to someone who's had a hard year?*

## The elevator pitch

**Ten seconds**
> Hiring judges you on a CV. We judge you on work you've actually done — and companies search for
> proof, not keywords.

**Thirty seconds**
> Most people now do two or three jobs inside one role, and their title picks one of them. So you
> end up invisible to the people who'd want you. On Disruptajob you describe real work you've done
> and tag what it demonstrates — that tagging is the only thing that moves your profile, there's no
> way to just claim a skill. Companies describe the actual work a hire would own, and we match the
> two. It's free for candidates, permanently. Companies pay.

**For an investor, add:**
> The business model is the moat. Indeed is paid per application and LinkedIn per recruiter seat —
> both of them need volume. We charge for access to proven capability. They can't copy us without
> cannibalising the revenue they already have.

## Headlines that work

Candidate-facing:
- Your job title doesn't do you justice.
- Two hundred applications. Four replies.
- It probably isn't you.
- Everyone sounds perfect on paper now.
- Show the work instead.

Company-facing:
- Find people by what they can actually do.
- Stop reading CVs. Start reading work.
- Describe the job, not the job title.

## The three promises, verbatim

Use these exact words. They're load-bearing and they're true.

1. **Free, always — companies pay, you don't.**
2. **Hidden until you say otherwise.**
3. **Start with one project, about ten minutes.**

## Objection handling

**"Isn't this just a portfolio site?"**
> A portfolio shows your work. This turns it into something searchable — a company looking for
> someone who's automated a messy process finds you because you've proved it, not because you used
> the word.

**"Nobody will write all that up."**
> That's the real risk, and it's why the first ask is one project, about ten minutes, with a
> question to help you start. We also show you what would make it stronger, specifically.

**"How is this different from LinkedIn?"**
> LinkedIn digitised the CV — same claims, better formatting, endorsements anyone can give anyone.
> Nothing on a LinkedIn profile has to be true. Here a capability score comes only from work you've
> described and tagged, and anyone can open the project and judge it.

**"What if my employer sees it?"**
> You start hidden. When you do open up, you can block your current employer by name — they can't
> find you in search, can't open your profile by link, and are never told you exist or that you
> blocked them. It's enforced in the database, not a setting we promise to honour.

**"How do you make money?"**
> Companies pay for access. Candidates never do — and not as a growth tactic: charging work-seekers
> is prohibited for platforms like ours under UK employment agency rules, so the model is built the
> only way it can be.

**"Isn't AI going to do this anyway?"**
> AI is why it's needed. It made polished applications free, so polish stopped being a signal.
> Proof of real work is the only thing left that's hard to fake.

## Cold outreach — candidate

Short, specific, no pitch deck. Send it from you, not from a product.

> Subject: your title doesn't do you justice
>
> Hi [name] — I'm building something for people whose job title covers about a third of what they
> actually do. You describe real work you've done, tag what it proves, and companies search for the
> proof rather than the keyword. You stay hidden until you decide otherwise, and you can block your
> current employer by name.
>
> It's free and always will be. Takes about ten minutes to put one project up. Would you try it and
> tell me where it's annoying? [link]
>
> — William
