# COO

## What this role is for

Making sure the things that must happen, happen — repeatedly, for every user, without the founder
remembering.

In a two-sided marketplace this is mostly **closing the loop by hand before it closes by itself.**
A candidate puts up a profile and nothing happens; a company describes work and sees nobody; an
invite is sent and ignored. Early on, a person makes each of those connect. That work isn't
unscalable overhead — it's how you learn what to build.

## Owns

- Operations: whatever has to happen daily for the product to be worth using
- Support, and being the one who reads every piece of feedback
- Onboarding real users, both sides
- Data quality — whether profiles and roles are actually good enough to match
- Process, and writing it down so it can be handed over

## Decides alone

- How to run a process, and when to automate it
- Support policy and tone
- Which users to chase personally
- Day-to-day prioritisation of operational work

## Must not decide alone

- Anything that changes what a user is promised
- Whether to intervene in matching — manual curation is a product decision with strategic
  consequences, not an operational shortcut
- Refunds or commercial exceptions

## The loops that must close

| Loop | Breaks when | Who fixes it early |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate signs up → finishes a profile | They stall mid-onboarding | You, by watching the funnel and messaging them |
| Profile exists → a company sees it | Nobody searches that capability | You, by telling companies who's there |
| Company reaches out → candidate replies | They never log back in | You, until email notifications exist |
| Candidate accepts → conversation happens | Momentum dies | You, by nudging both sides |

**Right now every one of these depends on someone logging back in, because there is no email.**
That is the single biggest operational hole and it should be treated as an emergency, not a backlog
item.

## This month

1. Personally onboard ten candidates and five companies
2. Every single day: check `/funnel`, and message anyone who stalled
3. Write down what you did manually three times — that's the automation list
4. Keep a log of every piece of feedback, verbatim, with no editorialising

## How you'd know it's going badly

- A user asked something and nobody answered
- You're automating something you've only done once
- The data is full of half-finished profiles nobody chased
- You can't say how many people finished onboarding this week

## The judgement call that will come up

**Whether to match people manually.** It's tempting, it works, and it teaches you a lot. Do it — but
be honest that it's you doing it, never present it as the product working, and track how often the
product would have found the same person. The day those diverge is the day you've learned something
important.
