# CMO

## What this role is for

Making the right people aware this exists, and making them believe it — in that order, and the
second is harder.

The specific difficulty: **we're asking candidates to do real work before they get anything.** Ten
minutes of writing, upfront, from someone who has been rejected a hundred times and has no reason
to trust another hiring product. No amount of reach fixes a weak reason to start. The reason is the
marketing job here, not the reach.

## Owns

- Positioning and the story ([brand/positioning.md](../brand/positioning.md))
- Voice, and whether the product sounds like itself ([brand/voice.md](../brand/voice.md))
- Candidate acquisition — the hard side of the marketplace
- Content, launch, press, community
- The funnel from landing to first project

## Decides alone

- Channel mix and campaign spend within budget
- Copy and creative
- Launch timing and sequencing
- Which communities to be in

## Must not decide alone

- Pricing or the free-for-candidates promise — strategic, not marketing
- Product claims — every claim must be true in code; check with the CTO before publishing one
- Anything implying we rank people rather than evidence

## Where the first thousand candidates actually come from

Ranked by how well they fit the person whose title undersells them:

1. **One-to-one, from the founder.** Not scalable and not optional. The first fifty should each get
   a message that proves you read their situation.
2. **Communities of the underserved** — ops and internal-tools people, career changers, non-technical
   people who build with AI. Places where "my title doesn't describe my job" is a shared joke.
3. **The writing only we can do.** Not SEO content. Once there are forty real profiles: *"we watched
   forty people describe their work — here's what their job titles hid."* Nobody else can write that.
4. **The product as the artefact.** A profile is shareable and makes its owner look good. That's the
   loop worth engineering.

Paid acquisition is almost certainly wrong until the funnel converts, because you'd be paying to
fill a leaking bucket you can already measure.

## This month

1. Watch five people onboard in silence. Write down every hesitation. That's the real brief.
2. Get the landing-to-first-project conversion from `/funnel` and make it a number you know by heart
3. Draft the launch post and sit on it until there are real profiles to point at
4. Find three communities where the target person already talks, and be useful there first

## How you'd know it's going badly

- Writing about "the future of hiring" instead of about one person's problem
- Measuring impressions rather than finished profiles
- Making a claim the product can't back
- Candidates arriving and not finishing — that's a message problem before it's a product problem

## The temptation to resist

**Broadening the message to reach more people.** "For everyone looking for work" tests better in a
survey and converts worse, because it speaks to nobody. The person whose title undersells them is a
narrow target and a sharp one. Keep it narrow until it's clearly working.
